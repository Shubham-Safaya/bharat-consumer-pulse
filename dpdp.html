DPDP-aware by design

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is not a copy of GDPR. It arrived after a decade of watching what compliance actually costs, and it makes some choices that matter for anyone building identity or audience systems for the Indian market. This page explains the ones that shaped how the synthetic demos here are built.

Consent as infrastructure, not paperwork

DPDP formalises the Consent Manager — a registered intermediary through which a person grants, reviews, and withdraws consent across data fiduciaries from a single interface. Consent is designed to be machine-readable, revocable, and portable, rather than a checkbox buried in each app.

For an identity system this means consent state is an event that arrives from outside your walls and can be withdrawn at any time. Modelling consent as a boolean at signup is structurally wrong under this regime; it has to be an event stream keyed to the resolved person.

Mobile number is the spine

The Indian data ecosystem resolves around the phone number: UPI payments, Aadhaar-linked services, banking, and messaging all key on it. Where Western identity graphs treat email as primary and phone as corroboration, the Indian reality inverts that.

That is why the synthetic identity demo here keys on a synthetic phone-hash. It is not a cosmetic choice — the whole match waterfall reorders when the strongest deterministic key is the number, not the inbox.

Purpose limitation with teeth

DPDP requires that personal data be processed only for the specified purpose the consent was given for, and deleted when that purpose is served. Combined with the Consent Manager's withdrawal mechanism, this pushes systems toward purpose-tagged storage and genuine deletion pipelines — the same deletion-as-a-graph-operation problem covered in the US identity work, now with a statutory clock.

Why a synthetic demo is the right way to show this

Demonstrating audience and identity mechanics on real Indian personal data would itself be a DPDP question. Building the demonstration on aggregate public statistics plus a fully synthetic population sidesteps that entirely: there is no personal data to have a lawful basis for, because there are no real people. The methodology is visible; the privacy exposure is zero. That is the point.
